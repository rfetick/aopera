Coronagraphic image
===================

From the residual PSD of a simulation, AOPERA computes the long-exposure image behind a perfect coronagraph, and its contrast curve.
This tutorial follows ``example/coronagraph_simulation.py``, and the code shown here is read from that script.
You should have done :doc:`tutorial` first.

----------

``CORO_ORDER`` is the order of the perfect coronagraph: 2 removes the piston from the pupil field, 4 also removes tip and tilt, and so on.

Coronagraphic image
-------------------

The output PSD is given at the wavelength of the WFS, so it is first scaled to the science wavelength.
The sampling grows with the wavelength: ``samp_sci`` is the sampling at the science wavelength, and the coronagraph must be built with it.

.. literalinclude:: ../example/coronagraph_simulation.py
   :language: python
   :start-after: #%% Coronagraph
   :end-before: #%% Plot

``coro.psf`` returns the image in units of a unit-energy PSF. ``coro.contrast_curve`` normalizes it by the peak of the diffraction PSF without coronagraph, and returns its azimuthal average against the separation in λ/D.
To normalize by the peak of an off-axis source through the coronagraph instead, use ``coro.raw_contrast`` with the PSD.
